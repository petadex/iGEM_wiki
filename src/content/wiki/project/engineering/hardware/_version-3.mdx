<EngineeringStage id="test">

**The OD calibration closes the largest V1 defect.** On **2026-09-01** the sensor was
calibrated against the donated spectrophotometer: **nOD tracks OD600 with a trendline
R² ≈ 0.99.**

<Figure
  src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6187/wiki/hardware-notebook/journal/claire-od-calibration-sep1.avif"
  alt="OD true vs reported chart"
  caption="Spectrophotometer OD600 against bioreactor nOD (2026-09-01). The trendline reaches R² ≈ 0.99 — the measurement V1 never had."
/>

Two caveats were recorded in the same run, and both matter: **taking the vial out and
putting it back shifts the reading**, because the gap between cap and holder admits varying
amounts of room light, and loose jumper wires dropped out mid-run (resolved by moving to
StemmaQT connectors). Changes beyond two decimal places are noise from light and LED
fluctuation.

A follow-up on **2026-09-06** established how little calibration is actually needed: a
**three-point calibration matches a ten-point one at R² = 0.993**, which is what the shipped
requirement now specifies.

<EngineeringStage id="learn">

**On 2026-08-31 Professor Milica Radisic joined as our first formal mentor and told us the
scale was wrong.** She accepted the low-cost high-throughput argument and the in-vessel
directed-evolution case, but judged the **40 mL vessel an order of magnitude too large for
the intermediate screen**, where the binding constraints are bench space and the volume of
enzyme required.

That replaced a single-scale platform with **two instruments that have different jobs**:

1. A **lower-volume screening tier**, to reduce a large pre-screened candidate set to a
   shortlist.
2. The **40 mL reactors**, retained for what they are uniquely suited to — long-horizon
   directed evolution, programmatic growth curves and gradients, and kinetics including
   Michaelis-Menten parameters and binding affinity.

It also converted a one-off review into a standing one: Radisic agreed to **monthly design
reviews** and began sourcing a fourth-year thesis student to develop the screening tier.

**What changed going into C1.4:** the platform became two instruments with different jobs
rather than one instrument compromising between them.
