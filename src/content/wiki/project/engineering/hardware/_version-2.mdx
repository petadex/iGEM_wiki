<EngineeringStage id="design">

**C1.2 — Subsystem builds to first integration.** We chose to validate each subsystem
independently against its own design of experiment before attempting integration, and we set
a **fixed integration deadline: July 30, all subsystems connected and a physical V1
prototype existing.**

A fixed date was deliberate. Subsystem work can expand indefinitely when nothing forces the
pieces together, and the failures we most needed to find were the ones that only appear at
the interfaces.

</EngineeringStage>

<EngineeringStage id="build">

**90% of the V1 bill of materials arrived 2026-07-13** — the first point in the season with
parts physically in hand, after prototype assembly had slipped across four consecutive
meetings. A wavelength-specific OD sensor was ordered **2026-07-06** rather than using a
general-purpose one. CAD was complete bar printing by **2026-07-20**. Transistor faults in
the pump drivers were diagnosed and fixed by **2026-07-27**, and a minimum-viable user
interface was running as a serial reader feeding a Python script. A UofT lab donated an
older spectrophotometer to serve as the OD calibration reference — which becomes the key
instrument in C1.3.

<EngineeringStage id="test">

**The 30 July integration hit its date**, with all subsystems physically connected. By
**2026-08-10** all subsystem electronics were consolidated and the Python host was reading
sensor data end to end, with the integration bugs in the JSON configuration resolved.

Integration then produced the track's most valuable artifact: **a defect list.**

- **One reactor failing stops every other reactor from reporting data.** A single point of
  failure that is tolerable at one unit and unacceptable at eight.
- **OD accuracy unverified.** The sensor was built and installed but never validated against
  a reference.
- **Port parsing for multiple reactors incorrect.**
- **Fan speed unresponsive between 1,800 and 2,800 RPM** regardless of setting.
- **Heating reached only ~56 °C** against the aspiration of 80 °C.

<EngineeringStage id="learn">

We wrote a **V1 Post-Mortem Analysis**, completed **2026-08-17**, mapping each defect to a
specific V2 change. The defects were **mechanical and architectural rather than
electronic**, which is precisely why V2 was scoped as mechanical redesign, fan replacement
and OD sensor work rather than a circuit revision — the post-mortem determined the shape of
the next iteration rather than producing a list nobody acted on.

**We also identified our real rate limiter, and it was not design capability.** **3D print
access** was named as the constraint on iteration speed. That is worth stating plainly
because it is the kind of bottleneck a team can work around once it is named and will keep
absorbing otherwise.

**What changed going into C1.3:** maintenance designed in from the start rather than
retrofitted, which is why serviceability becomes a first-class requirement in V2.

The single-point-of-failure defect and the pump PWM stall threshold exist in our records as

strength of the post-mortem and the integration notes.

</Callout>
