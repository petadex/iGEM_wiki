## The system specification

Most known plastic-degrading enzymes require laboratory conditions that do not reflect the
environments where plastic pollution accumulates. Although PAZy catalogued 495 validated
plastic-active enzymes as of October 2026, the best-characterised PETases still depend on
elevated temperatures, controlled pH, or pretreated substrates to achieve high activity.

Instead of engineering entirely new enzymes, we asked whether nature had already solved the
problem:

> **Do ambient-condition PETases already exist in environmental microbial communities,
> hidden within vast public sequencing datasets?**

**We hypothesised that they do** — and that the field's main challenge is not designing
better enzymes but *finding* them, through a systematic workflow for discovery,
prioritisation and experimental validation.

### What each track contributes

| Engine | Input | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dry Lab** | Public SRA assemblies and PAZy seed enzymes | Ranked candidate enzyme library |
| **Wet Lab** | Synthesised candidate library | Activity labels (active/inactive), then quantitative characterisation of the actives |
| **Hardware** | Candidate strains | Condition-specific activity dataset |

### Constraints

- Target **ambient-temperature** PET degradation rather than thermostable activity.
- Fixed *E. coli* as the chassis (May 2026).
- Maintain Registry compatibility through **BsmBI** Type IIS Golden Gate assembly.
- Candidate testing limited by wet-lab throughput and synthesis budget.
- Reactor capacity constrained by a single laboratory circuit.

### Assumptions and simplifications

- We use **copper release as a proxy** for PET hydrolysis — the assay measures copper
  concentration, not polymer mass.
- We use **BHET hydrolysis as a proxy** for PET depolymerisation.
- We define diversity at a **90% sequence identity threshold**, sampling sequence clusters
  rather than individual variants.
- Environmental metadata are sparse in public repositories: only **2.3% of SRA BioSamples
  (157,613 of 6,796,722)** include environmental measurements, so we infer temperature and
  pH from taxonomy and project metadata rather than measuring them per sequence.
- PlasticESM is built on **ESM-C 300M**, roughly half the parameters of the smallest model
  in our original plan.

### What we did not know at the start

1. Whether we could load copper into PET and reliably release it through enzymatic
   hydrolysis.
2. Where the induction window would fall relative to copper toxicity in the host strain.
3. Whether our ranking model would enrich for active enzymes beyond random selection.
4. Whether environmental metadata could meaningfully improve prioritisation despite the
   sparsity of public records.

## Engineering principles used

**Standards.** We standardised on Type IIS Golden Gate assembly with a deliberate split
between the two enzymes: **BsaI for the AS plasmid inserts** (pCusC, msGFP2, sul1, sul2,
dfrB10) and **BsmBI for the PD display arm and every PETase insert**, so the two assembly
levels cannot cross-cut one another. The split was fixed after we found internal BsaI *and*
BsmBI sites in the pUC19 prototype vector. Constructs maintain Registry compatibility, and
the library-design algorithm uses Kazusa *E. coli* codon tables as its default reference.
**Fifteen parts were submitted to the Registry in two collections** — the PETase Display
System Collection and the AS Plasmid Inserts Collection.

**Modularity.** Each reactor subsystem had a single owner and an independent design of
experiment. The enzyme library assembles from three interchangeable fragments, so designs
can be updated without rebuilding the entire construct. The reactor platform separates
high-throughput screening from detailed characterisation, letting each tier evolve
independently.

**Measurement.** We required positive and negative controls on every assay plate, with
cloning workflows the only exception. After discovering that short-read sequencing had
masked a plasmid-yield problem, whole-plasmid verification became the standard. We embedded
controls in the computational pipeline too, validating the corpus against 425 exact-match
known plastic-active enzymes rather than relying on bench controls alone.

**Reproducibility.** All analysis scripts read from and write to S3, eliminating dependence
on local files. Tool versions and database release dates are mandatory metadata, and any
analysis lacking version information is rerun rather than accepted. The library-design
algorithm produces seed-reproducible outputs, and the reactor platform ships with a complete
bill of materials and version-controlled firmware.

## What we would do next

**Wet Lab.** Confirm the mesh is PET by Raman spectroscopy before any further copper
experiments. Quantify copper release with ICP-MS rather than qualitative test strips.
Characterise the induction window with a full dose-response curve and archive the raw data.
Progress from BHET to polymer-film substrates. Resolve the sul1 verification question.

**Dry Lab.** Deploy the fused-LoRA fix and rerun training — roughly 20 GPU-hours, far less
than we assumed. Re-stratify the label split by both negative examples and label provenance
before reporting any enrichment metric. Separate label units into one prediction target per
task. Recover the 3,150 labels stranded on discarded sequence fragments. Populate a
per-sequence thermal-property feature, without which the ambient-temperature hypothesis
cannot be evaluated directly. Release the v260701 corpus or stop treating the rebuild as the
active corpus.

**Hardware.** Fix the OD sensor so live readings match its calibration, and recalibrate
across at least five concentrations before experimentation. Add fault isolation so a single
reactor failure cannot silence the array. Close the thermal gap between the per-reactor
limit and the temperature degradation experiments need. Build and characterise the
lower-volume screening tier. Improve 3D-printing access, which proved the real constraint on
iteration speed.

**Human Practices and Entrepreneurship.** Secure at least one letter of intent. Run the
product-market-fit survey described in our own manifesto. Quantify enzyme production cost, a
gap identified repeatedly and never closed. Record the rationale behind every scope
reduction, as our own governance rule required. Collect post-event feedback from attendees
rather than relying on registration counts.

**The loop.** Define a formal handoff specification with quantitative acceptance criteria
and a standardised return format for the labels Wet Lab sends back to Dry Lab.
