<EngineeringStage id="design">

**Cycle E — the loop's first turn.** The three engines only matter if they interconnect.
This cycle states exactly how far the loop got.

The handoff is specified in both directions. **Dry Lab** ranks candidates by predicted
activity, predicted thermostability and cluster prevalence, sizes the order to Wet Lab
throughput, and hands over sequences. **Wet Lab** clones and screens them against
FAST-PETase and a dead PETase **on the same plates**. **Activity labels return to the
model** to retrain the ranking head.

The last step is the one that makes it a loop rather than a pipeline, and it is the step
that has not happened yet.

<EngineeringStage id="test">

**In progress at the time of writing, and we are precise about which part.**

On **2026-10-05**, Thomas and Purav ran a halo assay at the Donnelly Centre to test the Dry
Lab model's predictions and to check whether the high-throughput assay works in principle.
**No result from that run is recorded** — no hit count, no image, no comparison.

**The screen on the ordered library has not been run.** The oPools only entered production on
2026-09-28 against roughly two weeks of synthesis, so the model-selected library could not
have been in hand for the October run. The feasibility of the assay and the test of the
ranked library are two different experiments, and only the first has been attempted.

What makes the pending test meaningful is that **its baseline is already fixed**. Published
prior work screened **161 natural homologs chosen at random**, expressed them in **yeast**,
and assayed them on BHET in a **384-colony halo assay**: **35 were active — a 22% hit rate** —
and HPLC on the top hits showed **two released more terephthalic acid than FAST-PETase, by up
to four-fold**. That 22% is the number model ranking has to beat, and the screen it comes
from was **random**, which is exactly what makes it a fair baseline rather than a competitor.

When the screen does run, the numbers that decide it are already specified: how many
candidates were screened, how many were active, and the resulting hit rate set against that
22% baseline, with FAST-PETase and dead-PETase controls on the same plates.

</EngineeringStage>

<EngineeringStage id="learn">

**This block is deliberately short, because the loop has not closed.**

The honest statement of status: the order was placed and went into production, a feasibility
halo assay ran in October without a recorded result, and **no activity label has yet returned
to the model.** The loop has turned far enough to place an order from a ranked list. It has
not turned far enough to change the model, which is the step that would make it a loop rather
than a pipeline.

We are stating that plainly instead of implying a closed cycle, because the one thing this
page cannot afford is a claim a judge can check and find hollow. The round-2 library *will*
be chosen by model uncertainty rather than predicted activity — testing the clusters the
model is least sure about, among those whose environmental metadata looks promising, so each
measurement lands where the current labels say least. That design is settled; its first
results are not in.

What B2.3 already tells us about this screen's value is concrete: only **4.7% of labelled
sequences** are among the clusters PlasticESM trained on, so labels returning from PETadex
sequences are exactly the data the model is missing.

</EngineeringStage>
