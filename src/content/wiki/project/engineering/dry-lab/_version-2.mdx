<EngineeringStage id="design">

**B1.2 — Metadata enrichment.** Sequence alone cannot tell you the conditions under which an
enzyme functions, and the project's whole premise is finding enzymes adapted to ambient
conditions. So the corpus needed environmental context, pursued along two tracks:

- **Environmental and taxonomic metadata** propagated from SRA BioSamples.
- **Activity data** extracted from the published literature.

<ProfileSteps />

</EngineeringStage>

<EngineeringStage id="build">

**Environmental track.** The first plan was to profile every library taxonomically with
Kraken2. We abandoned it on **2026-05-07** after estimating that processing 8.2 million
libraries sequentially would take on the order of a century — a runtime estimate made
*before* committing compute, which is the cheapest kind of failure. We switched to existing
SRA-STAT classifications, using Kraken2 only to fill gaps, and later adopted **BacDive**
growth records as the source of temperature and pH optima.

**Literature track.** We collected roughly 6,000 papers on plastic-degrading enzymes and
used a language model to extract activity data. An initial evaluation of Haiku on
**2026-08-25** was unsatisfactory, so we switched to Sonnet on **2026-09-01** at a total run
cost of about **$1,200**.

In parallel, roughly **25 volunteers** manually curated papers against a structured
extraction schema to build a human ground-truth benchmark. **We prohibited AI-assisted
curation for this effort** — using AI to build the benchmark would have destroyed its
purpose as an independent evaluation set.

</EngineeringStage>

<EngineeringStage id="test">

Three measurements, two of which found limitations.

**Extraction quality improved sharply** after a prompt revision and the addition of a
verification step. Measured 2026-08-25: decoy rejection rose from **65% to 100%**,
assay-variable extraction from **20% to 98.8%**, and activity-metric extraction from **18%
to 98%**. These compare two internal pipeline versions rather than performance against the
human benchmark, and no held-out test set was defined.

**The literature run was only partly usable.** More than 6,000 papers were processed by
2026-10-05, but **only about 30% of the output was immediately usable** because many
supplementary files were missing from the scrape.

**The human benchmark failed its own quality control.** On **2026-10-02**, independent
curators assigned to the same paper often disagreed by roughly 30%, or extracted
substantially different amounts of information. Until curator pairs reconciled those
differences, much of the benchmark could not support formal evaluation.

**Environmental coverage proved equally limiting.** Physical environmental measurements were
available for **157,613 of 6,796,722 BioSamples — 2.319%**. Temperature values covered
0.67% of samples, pH values 0.15%, and both were present in only 0.07%. Where temperature
existed, the median was 24 °C and the 99th percentile 60 °C.

<EngineeringStage id="learn">

**Numeric environmental metadata are too sparse to drive large-scale candidate
prioritisation — but categorical information scales.**

Aggregated at the cluster level, a dominant environmental profile could be assigned to
**14,455,359 of 18,172,960 clusters (79.5%)**. The resulting distribution differs markedly
from the clinically dominated view of the raw SRA data, becoming predominantly
environmental: soil 25.8%, marine 6.7%, freshwater 6.6%, sediment 5.4%, and **wastewater
sludge 740,110 clusters (4.07%)**.

That wastewater figure is the most directly actionable result for our intended application:
it expands a source set of **67,316 wastewater BioSamples into more than 740,000 candidate
sequence clusters**, a substantially larger search space for wastewater-adapted enzymes.

<ProfileStack />

**The temperature track hit a harder limit.** On **2026-09-01** we found that roughly 2,500
species in our metadata store could be queried against BacDive, but only about **1,200**
contained temperature information — against approximately **240,000 taxa** in the underlying
BioSamples. In practice the temperature feature extrapolates from roughly **1% of the
available taxonomic space** and remains blocked on a future BacDive recomputation.

**What changed going into B2.1:** we stopped treating temperature as a primary ranking
signal and shifted toward environmental categories and experimentally derived activity data.
Categorical environmental context scales across the corpus; direct temperature inference
does not.

<Callout tone="note" title="Two coverage numbers that are not the same thing">

The 2.319% figure is **directly measured** environmental metadata on BioSamples. The
BacDive-propagated estimates cover a much larger fraction of samples but are **inferred from
taxonomy**, not measured per sample. They answer different questions and should not be
quoted interchangeably.

</Callout>
