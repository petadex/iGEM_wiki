<EngineeringStage id="design">

**B1.1 — The PETadex corpus.** PETadex v1 originated from the Logan work in the Babaian Lab.
We set four requirements for what the corpus had to be, and the rest of this cycle is
measured against them:

1. **Versioned** — a named release, not a working file.
2. **Regenerable from source data** — rebuildable end to end, not patchable.
3. **Enriched with environmental metadata** — so candidates can be ranked on the conditions
   they come from, not sequence alone.
4. **Distributed with controls** — positive and negative, so the pipeline can be validated.

The fourth requirement came from a failure. An early embedding atlas contained a region we
called the *junkyard*, where sequences clustered in a way we could not explain biologically —
and we could not diagnose it, because the atlas had **no positive or negative controls at
all**. The issue was raised 2026-04-14 and was still open on 2026-08-13. A computational
resource without controls is as hard to debug as a bench assay without them.

</EngineeringStage>

<EngineeringStage id="build">

The published **v1.1 catalytic corpus, released 2026-10-15**, built through: a curated PAZy
seed set, expanded through NCBI nr, passed through profile HMMs we constructed ourselves,
searched against the Logan index, then open reading frame calling and hierarchical
clustering at 90%, 60% and 30% sequence identity.

The HMM layer is built from **609 curated reference enzymes** — the 495-record PAZy pull
curated to 473, unioned with an earlier 212-enzyme snapshot — clustered to **411 centroids at
90% identity**. Those resolve into **46 functional components**, segmented by mechanism into
**53 catalytic seed alignments**, each refined over four jackhmmer-style iterations.

**Sequencing the work mattered as much as the work.** A full Logan search costs thousands of
dollars, so HMM curation was deliberately front-loaded rather than running an expensive
search against an unfinished corpus. That curation added roughly **8 million sequences** and
new domain families before the large-scale search executed.

The funnel, measured rather than estimated:

| Stage | Count |
| --- | --- |
| ORFs matching a plastic-enzyme HMM | 307,155,746 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;from PAZy | 211 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;from NCBI nr | 4,723,128 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;from Logan / SRA | 302,432,407 |
| Distinct sequences | 123,966,734 (40.4%) |
| Clusters at 90% identity | 18,172,960 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;at 60% | 1,814,296 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;at 30% | 22,235 |

ORF length has a median of **308 amino acids** and a mean of **334**. Property calculation
failed for **18,683 sequences (0.006%)**, all containing noncanonical residues. At the 90%
threshold roughly half of all clusters are singletons — **9,169,319 of 18,172,960**.

**The positive control.** The corpus recovers **425 distinct PAZy plastic-enzyme identifiers
as exact MD5 sequence matches**, represented across 522 ORF entries, against the roughly 495
validated plastic-active enzymes catalogued in PAZy. That is end-to-end validation that the
search and recovery pipeline finds enzymes we already know are real. We state the caveat
rather than burying it: **all 522 recovered matches originate from the NR-annotated portion
of the corpus**, so this is the most favourable case and likely overestimates performance on
completely unannotated environmental sequences.

**The honest negative.** The `melting_point` and `thermostability` fields are **null across
all 307,155,746 rows**. The columns exist; no values were ever populated. The corpus
therefore contains no per-sequence thermal information — the very property underlying the
project's ambient-temperature hypothesis.

**A correction we are making publicly.** Earlier team materials described a corpus of 300
million sequences with complete catalytic triads. That does not hold up. The 100%-coverage
catalytic-residue field records what the matching HMM *expects* to find, not evidence that a
given sequence contains those residues. Verified per-sequence catalytic evidence exists for
**6,510,200 ORFs — 2.12% of the corpus** — at an HMM E-value threshold of 1e-5.

<CathForest />

Structurally the corpus resolves into **15 distinct catalytic CATH superfamilies** (plus Lcp,
which has no CATH code), dominated by alpha/beta hydrolases. The defensible description is not
"300 million PETases" but **putative plastic-degrading enzyme ORFs spanning PET,
polyurethane, polyamide, PHA and PLA chemistries**, with direct catalytic evidence for a
minority.

</EngineeringStage>

<EngineeringStage id="learn">

Two observations characterise this corpus better than any headline sequence count. The
**425 recovered PAZy enzymes** show the search pipeline reliably finds known plastic-active
enzymes. The **total absence of thermal annotation** shows the corpus cannot yet rank
candidates on the property the project cares about most.

Measured against the four requirements in Design, the one that bound hardest was
**regenerability**. A resource that cannot be regenerated cannot be validated, extended or
audited — which is why we rebuilt from scratch as **v260701** rather than continuing to
patch v1, and why on **2026-07-27** we adopted a project-wide reproducibility standard: all
scripts read from and write to S3 without relying on local files, tool versions became
mandatory, and database release dates became mandatory for resources such as InterProScan
and DIAMOND.

The junkyard failure and the PAZy positive control are the same lesson from opposite ends:
**build the controls before you need to debug.**

<Callout tone="warning" title="Version statement — resolve before publishing">

What is published is the **v1.1** catalytic corpus. The v260701 reproducible rebuild exists
in that release as a single annotation layer covering 6,619,107 distinct ORFs — **2.15% of
the corpus** — and 44.5% of its NR proteins postdate v1.1 and are absent from it. A reader
who combines "fully versioned reproducible rebuild" with "307 million ORFs" will conclude
something untrue. Describe v1.1 as the corpus and the rebuild as in progress; do not blend
them. Related: the Logan search completed around 2026-10-05 at 10 TB and 3.2 billion
sequences and is **not yet clustered**.

</Callout>
