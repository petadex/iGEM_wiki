<EngineeringStage id="design">

**A2.1 — Choosing the released signal and the loading chemistry.** The mechanism is simple
to state: load copper into PET, release it when a PETase hydrolyses the polymer, and detect
that release with a copper-responsive promoter driving either a reporter or a resistance
gene.

Three design choices, each with a reason and a date:

- **Copper over IPTG** as the released inducer. IPTG is a sugar and may decompose during PET
  printing above 200 °C; copper is thermally stable. *2026-05-23.*
- **Copper over terephthalic acid.** A TPA biosensor measures the MHET-to-TPA step
  specifically, which would bias selection toward enzymes good at that reaction rather than
  at overall PET degradation. TPA also risks diffusion-driven false positives over longer
  timescales and needs a strain and pathway we could not readily obtain. *2026-06-16.*
- **Copper as a separate solid phase** — a salt or powder rather than mixed into molten
  polymer — to avoid altering PET's melt properties or copper's oxidation state.
  *2026-05-23.*

We reversed one decision during development. Copper sulfate is the standard salt in
comparable published work, and anhydrous CuSO₄ was the initial target because hydrated
CuSO₄ can release water at printing temperatures and damage printed parts. On **2026-07-21**
we switched to **copper phthalate**, reasoning that its nonpolar aromatic structure should
incorporate more readily into similarly nonpolar aromatic PET, where CuSO₄ does not appear
to incorporate effectively.

</EngineeringStage>

<EngineeringStage id="build">

From **2026-06-02** we tested three loading routes: sprinkling copper onto PET,
melt-stirring copper into PET, and soaking printed PET parts in a copper-phthalate solution.

We ultimately **deferred copper-phthalate synthesis**. The drying step required evaporating
an aqueous solution on a hot plate, a procedure reported to crack glassware, and the
protocol we found looked unreliable enough that we could not justify running it. Instead, on
**2026-07-22**, we developed a copper-sulfate PET-fabric staining protocol as an interim
test system.

<EngineeringStage id="test">

Copper test strips, ordered **2026-08-05**, confirmed on **2026-08-11** that copper **can be
loaded into and released from PET mesh**.

We state the limits of that result plainly, because they matter: it is **qualitative only** —
one measurement date, no concentration measurements, no replicates, and no enzyme-free
control arm. A 45-minute copper soak on **2026-07-23** produced only weak or no colour
change. On **2026-08-24** we confirmed that passive copper leaching from loaded fabric does
not confound agar-plate assays, which at least rules out one alternative explanation.

<EngineeringStage id="learn">

As of **2026-08-13**, reliable copper incorporation into PET remained unsolved, and we could
not quantify copper release from copper-phthalate-loaded material because we never gained
access to ICP-MS instrumentation.

The honest statement of risk came on **2026-09-29**, when our mentor estimated a **30%
probability that the copper-mesh assay is theoretically impossible**, revising that to
**50%** within the same conversation. The concern is specific: PETase activity may never
release enough copper to activate the promoter, however well the rest of the system
performs. That discussion also identified a measurement trap — **copper mesh releases more
copper into growth media than into water**, so a simple leaching comparison cannot
demonstrate that enzymatic release is sufficient. We agreed **BHET** as a fallback substrate.

A second problem stayed unresolved: **the PET mesh may not be PET.** We flagged a possible
supplier error on **2026-09-29** and planned to confirm material identity by Raman
spectroscopy or FTIR. On **2026-10-05** that plan stalled — no team member had independent
access to the core facility, and the mesh appeared too thin for standard FTIR workflows,
which are optimised for powders. **We never confirmed the material identity**, which means
every copper result in this cycle carries an unverified assumption about the substrate.

What changed going into A2.2: we adopted a **gated validation workflow** in which each step
had to pass before the next began — antibiotic selection, then copper induction and pCusC
activation, then copper release from PET, then PETase-dependent survival, and finally
investigation of any unexpected surviving colonies.

</EngineeringStage>
