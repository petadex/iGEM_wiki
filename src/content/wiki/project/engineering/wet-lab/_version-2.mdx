<EngineeringStage id="design">

**A1.2 — Rebuilding PD.** After the surface-display route was deprecated in July, PD no
longer needed inducible expression from its pTac promoter. Rather than redesign the plasmid,
we left the construct unchanged and added **IPTG to all assay media**. At that point in the
season, changing the media cost less time, money and risk than starting another cloning
cycle. We made that call on **2026-07-20**.

On **2026-10-02** PD's original designer confirmed the rationale and two inherited features
we had deliberately left alone: a stop codon downstream of the linker, and an ATG codon
between the RBS and the INP anchor.

<EngineeringStage id="build">

On **2026-10-01** miniprep results showed strong DNA concentration and purity, and Golden
Gate assembly completed successfully, producing the full set of control PD plasmids.

Sequencing came back **2026-10-02** with a critical defect: **KLD editing had deleted
approximately half of the plasmid, including the entire display module.**

We ordered a replacement from GenScript together with pre-built control plasmids — we had
already identified GenScript on **2026-07-17** as having a stronger backbone catalogue than
IDT or Twist. It arrived more slowly than hoped: on **2026-10-08** GenScript estimated one
to two weeks, and the constructs reached the Donnelly Centre on **2026-10-13**.

<EngineeringStage id="test">

Before the replacement arrived we tested what we had, on **2026-10-07**. The result was an
unusually clean failure mode: cells carrying **AS alone grew as expected**, while
transformations containing **PD alone** or **PD + AS** produced **no growth** under
otherwise successful conditions.

<EngineeringStage id="learn">

The failure did not come from transformation conditions, media composition or antibiotic
selection — the AS-alone plate rules all three out. Sequencing traced it to the plasmid
itself: KLD editing had removed the display region PD needs to function.

Because repairing and re-verifying the construct would have meant another full cloning cycle
this late in the season, we abandoned repair and **replaced the plasmid entirely through
commercial synthesis**. The general lesson we took: once a construct's defect is confirmed
at the sequence level, buying a verified replacement can be faster than fixing one, and the
decision should be made on calendar time remaining rather than on sunk effort.

The whole-plasmid verification rule from A1.1 is what made this diagnosis quick — a year
earlier the same failure would have read as a transformation problem.

</EngineeringStage>
