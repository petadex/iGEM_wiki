<EngineeringStage id="design">

**A3 — Running the whole assay.** Three assay formats ran in parallel from May: **surface
display**, **selection** and **biosensor**. The reasoning, recorded **2026-05-06**, was that
too many unknowns remained to predict which would work, and the three shared enough
components that developing them together would not triple the workload.

The selection route changed shape along the way. A **2026-05-14** re-read of the Logan
preprint showed no auxotrophic strains had been used in its PETase work — the yeast strain
appeared only for plasmid maintenance — so on **2026-05-23** the nutrient-dropout design
became antibiotic selection, keeping the same logic: break PET to survive.

Surface display was **deprecated 2026-07-17**, on a structural risk rather than a
measurement. Evidence from **2026-05-24** showed PETases interact *dynamically* with PET
surfaces through hydrophobic interaction rather than binding one fixed site. A binding
module that tethers a cell to PET could therefore lose its attachment as the enzyme degrades
the very surface it is anchored to — the risk was assay instability, not failed degradation.

That left the biosensor and selection routes, and the question this cycle exists to answer:
**do they work together as one assay, rather than individually?** The design under test is
the integrated path — copper loaded into PET, released on hydrolysis, crossing into the
cell, driving pCusC, and producing either a fluorescent readout or a growth difference under
antibiotic selection — against the validation framework fixed on **2026-06-07**, which
separates PET from no-PET and copper from no-copper and compares FAST-PETase, IsPETase and
control constructs on the same plates.

</EngineeringStage>

<EngineeringStage id="learn">

**The biosensor arm answers A2.1's open risk, and the selection arm does not — yet.**

The load-bearing question from A2.1 was the mentor's 30%-then-50% estimate that PETase
activity might never release enough copper to cross the pCusC threshold. The biosensor plate
speaks to it directly: **M4-Q IsPETase grew *and* fluoresced** on copper mesh, which requires
copper to have been released, crossed the membrane and driven the promoter. That is the full
chain working once. The published result is careful to call it an **initial test**, and so
are we — one plate, one enzyme, no replicates, no dose-response.

**The selection arm carries a confound we should name rather than let a judge find.** All
test plates grew except Dead-IsPETase, which looks like clean enzyme-dependent survival. But
the surviving colonies were **morphologically similar to those on the sul1 copper-toxicity
plates** from A2.2 — the plates where growth was driven by copper tolerance, not by enzyme
activity. We cannot presently separate "survived because the enzyme released copper" from
"survived because this construct tolerates copper", because both produce growth on a copper
plate. The dead-enzyme control narrows it but does not close it: Dead-IsPETase differs from
the others in more than its catalytic residues.

**What this changes for the next iteration.** The biosensor is the readout to push on,
because its signal is positive and specific — fluorescence has no copper-tolerance
explanation. The selection arm needs a copper-dose-matched control before any hit from it
counts, and that control is cheap: the same plates at the same copper concentration with a
catalytically dead enzyme *and* an enzyme-free construct. Running the formats in parallel was
the right call in May, but by October the evidence says they are not equally trustworthy, and
we should say which one we would bet on.

<Callout tone="note" title="What this cycle does not claim">

We never generated a head-to-head comparison of the three original assay formats — that
dataset does not exist, and the surface-display decision was made on anticipated failure
modes and literature, not measured performance. No throughput figure is stated either: we
never measured wells or plates per day for any format. The only screening-scale target in
our records is 10,000 to 100,000 candidates screened for presence or absence of activity
with quantification on the positives, set 2026-09-22.

</Callout>
