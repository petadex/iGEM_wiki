### Build, Assembly, and Code

We modified the code to support RPM control using the fan's onboard tachometer. The speed measurements enabled adjustable fan RPM, including lower operating speeds that were not achievable in the first version of this subsystem.

The wiring was also adjusted to accommodate the additional pin on the new 4-pin fan, replacing the previous 3-pin configuration.

### New Design of Experiments (DoE)

No formal experiments were created specifically for this update. Instead, fan RPM was tested during subsystem integration for Version 2 (V2) and the final design.

### Metrics and Experimental Data

During integration, we tested the fan speed at varying powers (0-100% power). Fan speed was measured using the onboard tachometer. The slowest observed speed was ~200 RPM (at ~8%). The maximal speed was 6500 RPM. Thus, the acceptable RPM range for E.coli falls in the range of possible RPMs for the new fan design. However, we noted that during the operation, setting the fan to a power too low on the UI (&lt;8%) resulted in the fan completely stopping. The fan speed also appeared to vary slightly despite being set to a constant power. High speeds (> 2000 RPM) resulted in the stir bar no longer rotating in the vial. The rotation of the stir bar and the RPM of the fan were observed to not always be a 1:1 ratio.

## Next Steps

We aim to limit the user from selecting RPMs that are too high on the fan. The user cannot actually see the effect of the fan on the stir bar, so this limitation is important to prevent a long session where the user realizes that their stir bar was not mixing the entire time. Furthermore, we need tests to precisely calculate the min and max RPM of the fan that facilitate stirring. These next steps were listed as low priority, as acceptable stirring speed usually falls within a large range rather than a precise number. As such, some discrepancy or uncertainty in RPM values is not ideal but acceptable.
