### Main Function(s) + Requirements

<br />
This subsystem provides power to all bioreactor components, including high-power
ones like the 5W heater and the 2.5W pumps. The power requirement was calculated
from the power budget, tabulated below the requirements table.
<br />

| Requirement                                                                                                                                                                       | Priority | Why?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Provide power to the bioreactor** <br /><br /> Metrics & Criteria: Wattage in continuous 5V power<br /><br />Constraints: Must be able to deliver 15W of continuous power at 5V | High     | The power management system must be able to provide 5V 15W power to ensure functioning of the components (15W only supports operation of one pump at a time). <br /><br />Ideally, 20W of power should be supplied for simultaneous operation of all components, although more power headroom is always better. |
| **Fault protection**<br /><br /> Metrics & Criteria: Should be able to shut off in the event of a short circuit                                                                   | Medium   | This is required for safety, but should already be a feature integrated into commercial power supplies. As such, its priority in the bioreactor’s design is relatively low.                                                                                                                                     |

### Component Selection Process

Several alternatives were considered before we decided on the USB-based power supply system, including:

<br />
**One 150W power supply unit (PSU) for 8 bioreactors**

Rejected because of risk of operating in spill-prone wet lab environments, as these PSUs typically don’t
have closed housings and require additional high-voltage wire connections to
wall outlets. Also, the cost is higher compared to USB-based systems.

Rejected because of higher cost compared to USB-based systems. Also, barrel plug adapters that deliver 5V 20W+ are rare, and barrel plugs may be less intuitive than USB-C for users.

### Design of Experiments (DoE)

#### Power supply testing

A USB-C multimeter was purchased and used to monitor the current, voltage, and power drawn by the trigger board. These values will be monitored when high-power components (heater, pumps) are turned on simultaneously to measure the stability of the power supply.

    1. Set up the bioreactor and install the USB-C multimeter.
    2. Turn on the heater and 3 pumps.
    3. Monitor the voltage, current, and power readings for 30 minutes, and record the readings every minute.
    4. Turn off the heater and 3 pumps.

### Metrics and Experimental Data

#### Power supply testing

The voltage stayed within ±0.2V of the nominal value 5V. The power delivered was high during the first few minutes as the heater brought itself to 100oC, but after that the power became stable at around 12W. The power did not exceed 16W for the duration of the test.

For V2, the power supply will be optimized for voltage stability when powering
