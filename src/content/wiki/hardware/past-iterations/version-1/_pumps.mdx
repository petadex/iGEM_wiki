### Main Function(s) + Requirements

<br />
The peristaltic pumps subsystem controls dosing of liquids into and out of the
bioreactor. This meets the requirements of being able to pump a range of dosages
by being able to be controlled with different percentages of power. Multiple
pumps are also able to be used at the same time for transferring fluid into and
out of the bioreactor.

| Requirement                                                                                                                                                                   | Priority | Why (are we considering this)?                                                                                                                                                                                |
| :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Can pump a wide range of dosages with different PWM control values. The wider the range the better.                                                                           | High     | This function is necessary to maintain different flow rates for different dosage input and output of the pumps.                                                                                               |
| Multiple pumps can be used to transfer fluid into and out of the bioreactor. The more pumps that can be used the better, with a minimum of 2 pumps, one inlet and one outlet. | High     | This function is necessary so inlet and outlet pumps can be used at the same time to pump fluids in and out of the bioreactor.                                                                                |
| Easy to assemble & autoclavable. The less time taken to assemble and disassemble the better.                                                                                  | Medium   | The pumps should be easy to assemble and autoclavable for sterility purposes. This function is for accessibility purposes, but a longer assembly time does not interfere with the function of the bioreactor. |
| The pumps shall maintain a consistent flow rate while running. The less variability between flow rates the better.                                                            | Medium   | The pumps should maintain a consistent flow rate to not disturb the bacteria.                                                                                                                                 |

The Adafruit peristaltic pump was chosen because after looking into a few DIY options with stepper motors, this component is cheaper than making it ourselves. Just the stepper motor + motor driver individually that is required for the DIY pump would be almost the same price as the Adafruit pump, not taking into account the tubing and other components. The 5V pump was chosen over the 12V pump, since 5V power supplies are cheaper and more readily available.

### Build, Assembly, Code

The adafruit peristaltic pump works by squeezing a tube to allow liquid to flow at a constant rate. The DC motor turns a rotor with small motors, and as the rollers turn they squeeze the tube against the circular pump lid. The spot that is being squeezed moves liquid along, and as the roller leaves that spot, the tube allows more liquid to pass. This cycle allows for a constant flow supply.

The development of this subsystem is to validate the pump and its associated components before integrating with the rest of the bioreactor. At this stage, the pump is only tested on its own, with only superficial connections to the bioreactor. The main things to test in this stage are that PWM control works as expected, the flow rate is constant, and each component works as intended. This is so that any problems found during integration can be traced back to a different issue than the pump subsystem itself.

### Design of Experiments (DoE)

#### Quantitative experiments:

The first experiment was on making sure that there was a consistent flow rate, which was the mass flow rate in this case. A bowl was placed on a scale and I timed the pumps in increments of 30 seconds, measuring the total mass after each time interval. The values were pretty consistent from 60s onwards, but the 30s one is a little off. However, this could be due to the fact that during the experiment, there was still water left in the pump and after 30 seconds, there was not necessarily a consistent flow.

| Time (s) | Mass (oz) | Flow rate (oz/s) | Notes                                       |
| -------- | --------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 10       |           |                  | Too short                                   |
| 30       | 0.26      | 0.00867          | Run for 30 secs to get rid of water in pump |
| 60       | 0.71      | 0.0118           | Consistent                                  |
| 90       | 1.27      | 0.0141           | Consistent                                  |
| 120      | 1.75      | 0.0146           | Consistent                                  |
| 150      | 2.26      | 0.0151           | Consistent                                  |

The second experiment was on testing the variation of flow rates with different PWM values. For this, the time was kept constant but the PWM changed, and the mass was measured just like the previous experiment. The results showed that the flow rate varied consistently with the PWM as expected.

#### Qualitative Observations:

The pumps are housed in the circular lid. Since the pumps are designed to be squeezed by the rollers, they are very difficult to take out. As well, the lid itself has to be taken off of the motor before the pumps can be accessed. This raises a problem with autoclaving, as it is not very easily accessible.

#### Next Steps

Next steps for V2 include designing a more accessible cap for the pumps so the tubes could be more easily accessible for autoclaving purposes. As well, more experiments should be run to test the smallest PWM value possible, as it was seen that there was a lower limit to the PWM allowed for the pumps to run. As well, liquids with different viscosities will be tested to see if this changes the results of the experiments as well and to simulate real life conditions of the bioreactor.
