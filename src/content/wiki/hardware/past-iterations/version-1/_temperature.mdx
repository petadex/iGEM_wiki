### Main Function(s) + Requirements

<br />
Some microorganisms need temperatures above the ambient to survive and function.
The temperature control system regulates the temperature of the bioreactor to
allow these microorganisms to thrive. More specifically, the requirements for
this subsystem include:
<br />

| Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                  | Priority | Why?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Minimize temperature fluctuations once target is reached**<br /><br />Metrics: ±1.5°C total accuracy (±1°C control accuracy and ±0.5°C temperature probe accuracy)                         | High     | Temperature fluctuations can reduce bacterial vitality and affect experiment results.<br />Extreme overheating can denature proteins and kill bacteria.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Minimize time to achieve temperature** <br /><br />Metrics: Raises temperature from 25°C to 37°C in 1 hour <br /> <br />Constraints: N/A                                                   | Medium   | Criteria is based on what we saw while building a similar device, the pioreactor. <br /> <br />Additionally out of consideration for user-friendliness, we want to make sure that the bioreactor is as efficient as possible and can hit target temperatures asap to expedite experiments. |
| **Maximise heat range** <br /><br /> Metrics & Criteria: minimum range is at least 20-40C <br /><br />Constraints: Temperature range cannot melt surrounding components (estimate to be 70C) | Low      | The bioreactor should be able to characterise enzyme performance at a range of different temperatures because in the real world, climates are highly variable. Ideally, 70°C should be achieved to simulate extreme environments.                                                          |

<br />
To achieve this, we selected a feedback loop that consists of a heater, a
temperature probe, and a switch, where the heater is switched on and off by the
microcontroller based on probe feedback to maintain a target temperature.

<br />
**The DFRobot positive temperature coefficient (PTC) heater** as the heater,
chosen because:

- Self-regulates heater temperature at 100°C without external components
- Relatively low cost and is off-the-shelf
- 5W heater power provides decent power to a small reactor
- 5V operating voltage integrates well into the rest of the 5V system

<br />
**The DFRobot waterproof thermocouple** as the temperature probe, chosen because

- Sensor is waterproof and can directly measure the temperature of liquid inside
  the reactor
- Can withstand 120°C, allowing autoclaving between runs
- Has a digital interface, allowing easier and more reliable temperature readings
- Is off-the-shelf

<br />
**A mechanical relay** as the switch, chosen because:

- Capable of 3A current
- Gives audible feedback during switching, good for testing
  (Will be replaced by a MOSFET later)

The heater is placed below the vial and provides heat through a layer of thermal interface material. It is mounted above the fan (which drives the stir bar), on top of a stand made from heat resistant Onyx material. The stand both protects the fan from the high temperature of the heater and acts as an insulator to reduce heat loss from the underside of the heater.

<br />
The thermocouple is mounted on the cap of the vial, with the probe section
submerged in liquid to measure temperature. For the prototype, this is achieved
through the use of a rubber band, but in later versions a custom cap will be
created to mount the sensor.

This circuit reads data from the temperature probe and controls the heater via a relay. The heater and temperature probe draws power directly from the USB-C power supply.

#### Software

The control loop uses the Arduino PID library, configured to work for a relay. Instead of driving PWM, the PID output drives the heater active for a certain percentage of each 5-second window, allowing the relay enough time to switch on and off.

Temperature readings are done through the probe’s 1-Wire communication protocol, using the DS18B20 Arduino library.

Complete software (July 30): [Temperature PID Arduino Code](https://gitlab.igem.org/2026/software/toronto/hardware/-/blob/main/Temperature/Temperature_PID.ino?ref_type=heads)

### Design of Experiments (DoE)

<br />
#### Heater testing

The power of the heater, combined with the thermal characteristics of the entire
bioreactor, decides the maximum reachable temperature and the speed at which the
liquid heats. This experiment tests for both variables under different water
levels.

<br />
Procedure:

    1. Fill the vial with specified amount of water (21°C), and place the vial into the bioreactor
    2. Turn on the heater
    3. Record the temperature reading every minute for 60 minutes
    4. Repeat the experiment with a different water level

<br />
#### Control loop testing

This test measures the control accuracy of the control loop based on the Arduino PID library.

<br />
Procedure:

    1. Fill the vial with specified amount of water (21°C), and place the vial into the bioreactor
    2. Turn on the control loop and set a target temperature of 35°C
    3. Record the maximum temperature and minimum temperature (after reaching the target at least once) in 60 minutes
    4. Repeat the experiment with a different water level

#### Control Loop Testing

The experiment was performed with 15mL and 30mL water. Since temperature is sampled up to 3Hz in this experiment, raw data and plots were not preserved. Instead, a script was used to automatically log the highest and lowest temperature after achieving the target temperature.

The experiment was not continued after the first trial, as it became clear that the standard PID loop will not achieve the desired control stability. Deviations of up to ±2°C were observed in the first trial.

### Next Steps

In the next version, the control loop will be improved to allow less temperature
fluctuations. In future versions (beyond V2), a MOSFET will replace the relay to
allow silent operation, combined with an LED to indicate when the heater is
active.
