The Hardware team builds custom bioreactors that simulate industrial recycling conditions, enabling high-throughput enzyme testing across a range of temperatures and pH levels. By engineering the physical infrastructure for experimental validation, the team bridges the gap between the Dry Lab's computational predictions and real-world applicability, ensuring that promising PETase candidates can be rigorously tested under conditions that reflect practical biorecycling scenarios.

The dry lab team has assembled a database of thousands of PET-degrading enzymes. Meanwhile, the wet lab team has developed novel high throughput assays to validate their models. However, these screening methods can only measure enzymatic activity under one condition. Full characterization requires quantifying their behaviour in multiple temperatures, pH values, and media. This ground-truth data can allow drylab to improve their models over time.

So how do we close the gap? This year, iGEM Toronto’s hardware team introduces a new bioreactor that is accessible for wet lab researchers, both in terms of its user interface and its assembly, that can work in parallel with up to 96 other bioreactors, and contains sensors and actuators that provide the basic functionalities including temperature regulation, OD600 readings, and stirring, all for a total price less than $250. Stakeholder interviews with wet lab members, engineering experts, and even CEOs informed our user interface, which connects to bioreactors using an intuitive USB serial connection. Plug it directly into your laptop and a wall socket, and the bioreactor is ready for usage.

The most recent design uses three peristaltic pumps: two inlet pumps and one outlet pump for pH control, optical density (OD) control, and extracting fluid. It also uses one heater, to reach a peak temperature of approximately 60 degrees Celsius, and maintains temperature to within one degree of an accepted thermometer value. The stirring is completed by a stir bar and PC fan. The overall design is powered by a USB-C charger and the current USB hub can house up to 8 bioreactors per laptop USB port. iGEM hardware’s bioreactor makes testing the plastic-degrading activity of PETases possible, but its features make it an accessible, user-friendly, and highly efficient solution for anyone hoping to automate their assays.

| Temperature     | Maximize heat range                                                            | At least 20–40 °C, ideally 70 °C; must not melt surrounding components (~70 °C)    | Low      | Enzyme performance should be characterised across temperatures because real-world climates vary; 70 °C simulates extreme environments.                                                                                                                            |
| Temperature     | Minimize time to reach temperature                                             | 25 °C → 37 °C in under 1 hr                                                        | Low      | Heating time is short compared with experiments that run for days or weeks, and no user intervention is needed after setting the target, so it has little impact on user experience.                                                                              |
| Power           | Provide power to the bioreactor                                                | 15 W continuous at 5 V (ideally 20 W)                                              | High     | 15 W at 5 V is needed for the components to work, but only supports one pump at a time. 20 W would allow all components to run at once; more headroom is always better.                                                                                           |
| Power           | Provide stable power to the Arduino Nano                                       | Regulated 5 V via the 5V pin, or stable 7–12 V via VIN                             | High     | A power-induced reset or crash can interrupt long experiments and wipe commands and data logs.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Power           | Fault protection                                                               | Shuts off in the event of a short circuit                                          | Medium   | Required for safety, but already built into commercial power supplies, so it ranks relatively low.                                                                                                                                                                |
| Pumps           | Easy to assemble and autoclavable                                              | Takes less time to assemble than the previous prototype                            | High     | Prototyping showed the Adafruit pump tubing was hard to remove for assembly and autoclaving, so V2 focused on easy assembly and disassembly.                                                                                                                      |
| Pumps           | Withstand forces against the walls while running                               | The longer it holds together while running, the better                             | High     | The new V2 pump lid comes apart at the sides, so it must hold together under running forces.                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Pumps           | Pump a wide range of dosages with different PWM values                         | The wider the range, the better                                                    | High     | Needed to maintain different flow rates for different input and output dosages.                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Pumps           | Use multiple pumps to move fluid in and out                                    | Minimum 2 (one inlet, one outlet); more is better                                  | High     | Inlet and outlet pumps must be able to run at the same time to move fluid in and out.                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Pumps           | Pump fluids of different viscosities                                           | The more viscous the fluid it can pump, the better                                 | Medium   | Different reagents pumped in and out may have different viscosities.                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Pumps           | Maintain a consistent flow rate while running                                  | The less variability, the better                                                   | Medium   | A consistent flow rate avoids disturbing the bacteria.                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| OD              | Report bacterial growth as a normalized multiple of the starting density (nOD) | Default output; no calibration needed                                              | High     | Quantifying change in bacterial growth is the key function of OD readings, and nOD needs no calibration, so it is the bioreactor's default.                                                                                                                       |
| OD              | Calibrate to any user's environment                                            | Accounts for differences between vials, lighting/LED drift and cultures            | High     | Lab testing showed external factors affected precision. At 2-decimal accuracy, nOD couldn't detect the small changes a spectrophotometer can, so better calibration improves versatility.                                                                         |
| OD              | Plot nOD to show the log growth phase                                          | nOD plotted live during the experiment                                             | High     | Users previously had to plot results in external apps after the experiment. Stakeholder feedback asked for this to be built into the bioreactor to make it more scalable and less dependent on the user's own tools.                                              |
| OD              | Run 2 photodiode sensors at the same time                                      | Reference + collecting sensors; I2C address conflict resolved                      | High     | A reference sensor accounts for environmental drift. The two sensors are identical, so differences come from the environment rather than the hardware. Two photodiodes are standard in bioreactors.                                                               |
| OD              | Stable, simplified I2C wiring                                                  | Plug-in connector per sensor (Stemma QT) instead of 8 jumper wires                 | Medium   | In V1, jumper wires disconnected whenever the bioreactor was moved, and users had to rewire 8 connections each time.                                                                                                                                              |
| OD              | Translate nOD into OD600                                                       | 3-point quadratic calibration against a spectrophotometer, converted automatically | Medium   | V1 required manual conversion in external apps; V2 automates it. It is Medium because not all experiments need OD600 and nOD is the default.                                                                                                                      |

| Data & Controls | Bioreactor data are displayed on a user interface                              | Shows at least temperature, pH and OD; values may occasionally be stale            | Medium   | Expected feature, but data and controls can still be transmitted without a UI.                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Data & Controls | User interface is user-friendly                                                | Legible text, easy-to-find buttons, easy navigation; built with free tools         | Medium   | V1 required command-line prompts and reading the serial line, which made it unapproachable.
