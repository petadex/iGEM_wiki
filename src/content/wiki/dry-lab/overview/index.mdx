## Search

Enzymes that break down plastic offer a cleaner way to deal with plastic waste, but the field has been working from a very small pool. Only around 500 have been validated, and new ones have mostly been found piecemeal, one enzyme and one database at a time. PETadex does this search systematically and at scale, expanding this enzyme family as far as public data allows (mining NCBI-nr and the SRA, which are both public repositories). Starting from a curated set of reference enzymes, we searched public protein databases and Logan, an assembly of the world's public sequencing data. We recovered over 300 million related sequences from diverse environments across the globe, ranging from bottle-recycling plants in Japan to the 400-year-old remains of a Spanish soldier, and covering enzymes for many plastics beyond PET.

This scale matters. In an early random screen of just 161 of these sequences, two outperformed FAST-PETase, the field's current benchmark. That suggests many more strong enzymes are waiting in the full set.

## Annotate

Not every relative or homolog will work, so we then annotated each sequence in depth, checking for key catalytic features, structural family, and source environment (among many other features) to narrow the space to sequences most likely to function. Most sequences also carry the temperature and pH of the place they came from. That lets us look for enzymes suited to real-world conditions, such as a cold wastewater plant, rather than only those that work on a lab bench.

## Predict and test

Finally, a machine learning model trained on published activity data predicts how active and stable each enzyme should be, turning a catalogue of candidates into a ranked shortlist for lab testing. Lab results feed back into the model, so each round of testing sharpens the next set of picks.

## Open

Everything is open. Anyone can search the full database on petadex.net in under a minute, and our model and training data are freely available. The result is an end-to-end framework for discovering and prioritizing enzymes, applicable to any enzyme family.
