## Why profile BioSamples

<Placeholder>

Most PETadex sequences come from metagenome runs, and the BioSample record attached to each run is the only description of where it was collected. That record is free text and usually incomplete. To use it as a model feature we need it in a fixed vocabulary, and we need to know how much to trust each label. The profiles do both, and they never read the organism's species name, so a profile describes the sample's environment and not what grew in it.

</Placeholder>

## Where the samples are vs where the sequences are

<Placeholder>

Counting BioSamples and counting sequence clusters give opposite pictures. Human-body profiles are 59.2% of the BioSamples that contribute a PETadex sequence but only 1.4% of the clusters, while natural environments go from 13.2% to 76.3%. Soil alone holds 11.5 times more clusters than its sample count suggests. As an independent check, the profiles also separate by an environmental temperature estimated from the microbes present rather than from the metadata: gut profiles sit near 37 °C and soil and freshwater near 31 °C. The effect is real but small.

</Placeholder>

## Known rule defects

<Placeholder>

Building the interactive chart turned up four defects in the profile rules. They are not yet fixed. Together they affect about 1.5% of profiled BioSamples, and the samples-to-clusters flip does not depend on any of them. Fixing them means re-running the profile build and the propagation, after which every number shifts slightly.

1. **Coral is read as oral.** The pattern for `oral metagenome` has no word boundary, so it also matches `coral metagenome`. About 2,035 coral-reef BioSamples are labelled Human oral.
2. ***Salmonella* is read as salmon.** One rule reads the species field, and the animal-host term `salmo` matches *Salmonella*. About 4,800 `human, stool` samples become Animal gut. This also breaks our own rule that species never decides a profile.
3. **Body-site rules do not check the host.** At least 30,682 animal samples carry human labels, such as bovine faeces, dog urine and cattle respiratory samples.
4. **A bare "metagenome" label wins.** A metagenome label of just "metagenome" hits the Other environmental catch-all before more specific fields, such as `env_medium = lake sediment`, are read. Lake Baikal sample SAMN17126602, which states 0 psu, is profiled Other environmental.

</Placeholder>

## Other limitations

<Placeholder>

- **Half the samples are unprofiled.** 50.6% of BioSamples carry no environment signal, so profiled data leans toward well-described studies.
- **The temperature check is small.** ε² = 0.044. The temperature estimate also tops out near 37 °C, because BacDive's recorded optimum is often an incubator setting, so host profiles pile up there.
- **The popup's explanation is a reading, not a measurement.** That low-ratio profiles are isolates of the same few organisms sequenced again and again fits the one human stool isolate we inspected, whose 13 sequences all sit in clusters of about 33,000. We have not measured the isolate share per profile.

</Placeholder>
