<Callout title="Methods for PETadex-PAZy">
This page is the full write-up behind the [PETadex-PAZy Reference Curation](/dry-lab/model/#petadex-pazy-reference-curation) subsection of the Model page. The code and data are on GitHub at [Pixeldom4/plasticome](https://github.com/Pixeldom4/plasticome).
</Callout>

## Why curate?

The breadth and diversity of existing plastic-degrading enzyme databases is very constrained. The plastics-active enzymes database (PAZy) contains 495 experimentally verified plastic-active enzymes, most of them being homologs of leaf-branch compost cutinase (LCC) or IsPETase. Using established homology-based retrieval and expansion pipelines, we built a workflow for the retrieval of plastic-degrading enzymes from the SRA as well as the NCBI-nr by leveraging the Logan assembly.

PETadex-PAZy is the part of PETadex that links back to characterized enzymes. Everything else in PETadex is a putative enzyme that hasn't been tested in the lab.

Every downstream search starts from these sequences, so mistakes here carry through. An enzyme that shouldn't be there pulls in false relatives, and a missing one leaves a whole family unsearched. That's why we checked each record against its source paper before running any search.

## Building PETadex-PAZy

To generate this updated PETadex-260701 dataset, a starting search query was created by combining two versions of the PAZy database: the first from the original PETadex generation (pazy-240826; *n* = 212; 26 August 2024), and a recently updated version (pazy-260701; *n* = 473; retrieved 30 June 2026). Sourcing 136 enzymes from pazy-240826, 397 enzymes from pazy-260701, and 76 merged between both generates a 609-enzyme union set: PETadex-PAZy. PETadex-PAZy was then clustered at 90% amino acid identity to 411 cluster centroids.

## Curating pazy-260701

pazy-260701 was retrieved on 30 June 2026; 260701 is the version label of the dataset, not the retrieval date. The 495 records in the 2026 snapshot were curated manually against their source publications: 26 records without experimentally reported activity were removed, and 4 sequences were added (one reported in the literature but absent from PAZy, one replacing an inactive PAZy record, and two that PAZy aggregates under a single identifier), leaving 473 enzymes.

Of the 495 records, 175 were already in the original PETadex seed set, 287 were new, and 33 came with no associated sequence. The curator checked that the paper reports the enzyme, that the accession points to the protein the paper tested, and whether the paper shows any direct activity on plastic rather than only indirect. Each decision is recorded in sheet `pazy-260701_manual-curation` by cell colour.

| Decision | Marked as | Records |
|---|---|---|
| Sequence taken from the accession | Green, sequence column | 470 |
| No sequence could be recovered | Red, sequence column | 26 |
| Only a fragment is available | Yellow, sequence column | 3 |
| Accession added or corrected | Green, accession column | 28 |
| Name changed to the one in the paper | Green, name column | 6 |
| Direct evidence of activity | Activity column | 459 |
| Indirect evidence of activity | Activity column | 14 |
| No experimentally reported activity (removed) | Activity column | 26 |

For 53 records the curator left a note on why something was changed, and for 21 a note on where the sequence came from when it had to be found by hand, e.g. translating a DNA sequence from a supplement, finding a gene in a genome from its PCR primers, or copying a sequence out of a patent. A few examples:

|---|---|

| Mors1 | Changed the accession to the accession from the paper. |

| Est1 | BAI99230.2 = Est119 |
| CalB | The first two citations on PAZy didn't mention CalB exactly, but third does, so I removed the first 2. |

## Preparing pazy-240826

The first snapshot, pazy-240826 (26 August 2024; *n* = 212), is the seed set of the original PETadex build. It appears as plasticome v1 (213 records) in the workbook, and, after cleaning, plasticome v1.1 (212 records), the version merged here. Cleaning involved four changes:

1. **Accessions.** 13 records had their accession corrected to the one that matches the reported protein.
2. **Erickson sequences.** The 21 metagenomic enzymes from Erickson et al. (2022) were reset to the sequences in the paper's supplementary table D1 (below).
3. **DOIs.** Every record now carries the DOI of its source paper (212 of 212).
4. **Labels.** Kingdom was written several ways ("Bacteria", "Metagenome", "Archaea", "Eukaryota", "Eukaryotic", "Eukarya"); these were normalised to superkingdom during annotation.

## Merging

We joined the two snapshots on accession, ignoring the version suffix. This gives a union of 609 enzymes: 397 only in pazy-260701, 136 only in pazy-240826, and 76 in both.

| Source tag | Meaning | Records |
|---|---|---|
| pazy-260701 only | pazy-260701 record with no match in pazy-240826 | 397 |
| Both snapshots | Accessions match across the two snapshots | 76 |
| pazy-240826 only | pazy-240826 record with no match, appended | 136 |
| **Total** | | **609** |

**Duplicates are kept on purpose.** When the two snapshots cite the same enzyme under different accessions, nothing joins them and the enzyme is documented twice. Cut190, for example, is in pazy-240826 as `BAO42836.1` and in pazy-260701 as `WP_015787089.1`. These are not resolved by hand: identical and near-identical sequences are collapsed by clustering (see [Outputs](#outputs)).

## Sequences

Instead of copying sequences from an older table, we pulled each one fresh by its accession. Each accession was looked up against NCBI Protein, UniProt, RCSB PDB, NCBI Nucleotide (for coding sequences), MGnify and UniParc, in that order, and we took the first hit. All 565 distinct accessions resolved.

| Database | Accessions resolved |
|---|---|
| NCBI Protein | 473 |
| UniProt | 53 |
| UniParc | 26 |
| MGnify | 6 |
| RCSB PDB | 4 |
| NCBI Nucleotide (coding sequence) | 3 |
| **Total** | **565** |

Wherever we could retrieve a sequence, the retrieved one wins. We only carry a sequence over as-is when there's no accession to pull from: 13 pazy-240826 records whose sequence only exists in their paper (12 of the Erickson enzymes, plus jmPE13), and 28 pazy-260701 records that keep the sequence from their PAZy entry.

So the paper's sequence only survives for 12 of the 21 Erickson enzymes. The other nine have an accession, either in pazy-240826 or through a pazy-260701 record they merged with, so they use the retrieved sequence.

## Annotation and structures

We then joined each record back to its source tables to pull in substrate, organism, superkingdom, metagenomic origin, UniProt accession and PDB structure. Where the two snapshots disagreed, we went with pazy-260701. Some PDB IDs in the sources were typos or obsolete, so we checked each fix by aligning the RCSB sequence against the record's own sequence. After these fixes, 91 records link to an experimental structure.

We predicted a structure for every enzyme with ESMFold2. Across the 609 enzymes the mean pLDDT was 0.94, and 605 (99.3%) had a mean pLDDT of at least 0.70. These structures are what goes into [CATH Assignment](/dry-lab/model/#cath-assignment).

## Outputs

The 609 sequences were clustered at 90% amino acid identity with USEARCH v11.0.667 in a reference-seeded scheme. Thirteen well-characterized enzymes were fixed as cluster centroids, and the remaining sequences were first recruited to these centroids. Those left unassigned were clustered *de novo* with centroids chosen by decreasing length. This produced 411 clusters (13 reference-seeded, and 398 *de novo*). The 411 centroids are the query set for the NCBI-nr search. Separately, collapsing PETadex-PAZy at 100% identity gives 493 non-redundant proteins, which were used to seed the profile HMMs.

| Set | How it is made | Size | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| PETadex-PAZy (union) | pazy-260701 and pazy-240826 merged on accession | 609 | The reference record of every enzyme |
| Non-redundant | Collapsed at 100% identity | 493 | Seeding the profile HMMs |
| 90% centroids | Reference-seeded clustering at 90% identity | 411 | The NCBI-nr search query, and [Component Assignment](/dry-lab/model/#component-assignment) |

In the non-redundant set, 384 sequences come from a single record and 109 are shared by two or more. The 411 centroids fall into 46 components (see [Component Assignment](/dry-lab/model/#component-assignment)).

## What "validated" means

An enzyme is in PETadex-PAZy because a published paper reports it working on a plastic. We didn't test these enzymes ourselves, and the strength of the evidence varies between papers: most were purified and tested directly, while a few only have indirect evidence (e.g. a sample was shown to degrade plastic, but the activity wasn't narrowed down to a single enzyme). The search still treats every PETadex-PAZy enzyme the same way.

Most of PAZy's enzymes are homologs of LCC or IsPETase, so the families the pipeline searches most thoroughly are the ones already best studied.

## Reproducing

The curation workbook, `plasticome.v2.xlsx`, holds every sheet named on this page. The union is rebuilt from the two cleaned tables by the first step of the PETadex-PAZy pipeline:

```bash
python3 "lib/01 union/build_union.py" \
    source-data/plasticome.v1.1/plasticome.v1.1.tsv \
    source-data/plasticome.v260701/cleaned_pazy-260701-singletons.tsv \
    -o runs/<run>/01-union.tsv
python3 "lib/01 union/fetch_sequences.py" runs/<run>/01-union.tsv
```
