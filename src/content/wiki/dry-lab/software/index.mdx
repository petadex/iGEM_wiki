## LOBSTER

{/*
  Demo D1 (MVP). ~900 words.

  with built-in degeneracy); validation, including the IsPETase/LCC
  literature controls; install and run, step by step.
  Demo: one preselected cluster of 8 to 12 sequences, slider over degeneracy
  or fragment count, output fragment library table, variant count, naive
  cost vs LOBSTER cost. Frozen JSON from a real pipeline run, under 200 KB.
  VERIFY the ">50% nucleotide savings" figure before publishing it.

To maximize our Wet Lab activity validation efficiency, we developed the LOBSTER (Library Optimization for Bioprospecting Sequences Through Economical Recombination) algorithm. Rather than naively purchasing full-length DNA sequences for each PETase core, LOBSTER splits the full-length sequence into sections called fragment positions. LOBSTER then designs fragments at these positions to be combinatorially assembled using Golden Gate assembly and degenerate codons. Overall, LOBSTER seeks to maximize the number of PETadex natural PETase cores which are generated in the resulting full-length library, which we define as coverage, under library size and nucleotide constraints. By running LOBSTER on 90pid clusters, we can experimentally validate them for a fraction of the nucleotide cost (~45%), greatly expanding our full validation space.

<Figure

  alt="Overview of the LOBSTER algorithm: full-length PETase cores are split into fragment positions, and fragments with degenerate codons are designed at each position for combinatorial Golden Gate assembly into a full-length library"
  caption={<><strong>Figure 1.</strong> Overview of LOBSTER. Each PETase core is split into fragment positions, and fragments designed at each position, with degenerate codons, assemble combinatorially by Golden Gate into a full-length library that covers as many natural cores as possible.</>}

LOBSTER operates by isolating the PETase cores, identifying and selecting Golden Gate cut sites, then greedily adding cores to the solution using additional fragments and degenerate codons. Using this approach, the LOBSTER output designs reduce the purchased nucleotides by 55% to cover the LCC and IsPETase 90pid clusters. This expands our PETase activity validation search space across more clusters. Additional details on LOBSTER's implementation can be found on its own page.

Researchers often come to our database and ask a simple question.

> I have this novel enzyme here that I know nothing about. Can you tell me what it is?

We can tell what it is by looking at the enzymes that are similar to it using the entirety of the PETadex corpus. We've built this into a lightweight tool in the web-browser, no installs and less than a minute runtime.

<ArchitectureFigure

Nothing runs between searches, so the tool costs almost nothing while it waits for a query. When one arrives, a coordinator starts one worker per shard, and a final step merges their hits.

## Sequence record card

{/*
  Demo D3 (MVP, cheapest, build first). ~350 words. Shared with the Model

From our vast dataset of millions of putative plastic-degrading enzymes, it is important to not lose track of the minute details of each sequence. To explore these details, we've given each ORF (sequence) in PETadex its own page, documenting everything that we've annotated for these enzymes.

We've annotated everything from the structures of these enzymes, down to their catalytic mechanisms and which residues in the sequence are specifically responsible. In addition, we've included metadata features, such as where each of these sequences comes from, its homologous neighbours and how they fall within our hierarchical clustering. We've even included how we ordered these sequences with the LOBSTER algorithm and even the readout we got from our Wet Lab validation of these enzymes!

Check out the demo below to see how this looks on two reference enzymes, and see [petadex.net](https://petadex.net) for the full pages.
