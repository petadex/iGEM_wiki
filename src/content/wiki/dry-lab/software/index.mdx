## LOBSTER

{/*
  Demo D1 (MVP). ~900 words.

  Demo: one preselected cluster of 8 to 12 sequences, slider over degeneracy

  VERIFY the ">50% nucleotide savings" figure before publishing it.

To maximize our Wet Lab activity validation efficiency, we developed the LOBSTER (Library Optimization for Bioprospecting Sequences Through Economical Recombination) algorithm. Rather than naively purchasing full-length DNA sequences for each PETase core, LOBSTER utilizes golden gate fragment assembly and degenerate codons to create efficient fragment pool designs that can be combinatorially assembled to form a library of full-length cores. LOBSTER seeks to maximize the number of PETadex natural PETase cores which are generated in the resulting full-length library, which we define as coverage. To keep the generated libraries experimentally feasible, this coverage optimization is run subject to nucleotide quantity and library size constraints. Additionally, since the full cores are combinatorially assembled, golden gate assembly will create cores not found in the PETadex. While these chimeras of naturally-occurring mutations were not identified through natural sequencing, it is still plausible that they show plastic-degrading activity. This further expands our Wet Lab validation space of potentially active PETases.

## ESM Atlas

{/*
  Demo D4 (High, build only if D1 to D3 land early). ~350 words.
  <AtlasMap /> below is the live atlas and calls the petadex.net API. Swap it
  for the frozen-data version if the external content check rejects it.
  Covers: what the atlas shows, how the map is produced from protein
  language model embeddings, metadata search, links through to sequence
  pages.
  Demo: family centroids downsampled to about 2,000, canvas scatter, hover
  for metadata, colour by one axis. Under 500 KB.
*/}

To allow for exploration of PETadex enzymes in a more intuitive manner than alignment search, we built an Atlas of UMAPed embeddings of PETadex enzymes, modelled off of https://biohub.ai/esm/protein/atlas. This atlas provides an accessible way for users to see the scale of PETadex, even for those not familiar with biology. The embeddings capture rich information about the proteins themselves: colouring along metadata terms shows clustering along CATH domains, domains, and phylum, showcasing the diversity of PETadex. Clicking on any point on the atlas takes the user to a page for that family (for example https://petadex.net/family/23828/) providing a quick summary glance of that 30% cluster, including metadata terms associated with its centroid.
