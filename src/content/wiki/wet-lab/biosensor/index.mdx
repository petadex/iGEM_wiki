The Biosensor assay is a plate screening method which uses fluorescence to identify PETases that can hydrolyze PET. A library of PETases are transformed into E. coli, and are surface displayed. A PET mesh embedded with copper is put on top of these plated E. coli, and the copper molecules released by PET degradation induce expression of msGFP2, allowing for colony identification. A fluorescent colony indicates that the PETase expressed by that colony can hydrolyze PET enough to release the copper. The colony can then be sequenced to determine the successful PETase sequence.

## Fluorescence

The assay provides a visual binary outcome of PETase success through colony fluorescence. msGFP2 was selected as the fluorescent protein to express when copper is detected, indicating a successful PETase, as it is a well-characterized GFP that folds efficiently and is photostable [@valbuena2020photostable].

It should be noted that colony fluorescence cannot be quantified beyond a binary output due to limited equipment access. However, colony fluorescence may not necessarily indicate a high-performing PETase; it may also reflect PETase expressed at high levels, where higher enzyme amounts increase catalytic activity and inducer access.
