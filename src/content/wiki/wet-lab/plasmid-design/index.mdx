Two sets of plasmids were designed: the PETase Display (PD) plasmid, used to display PETases on the surface of E. coli, and the Assay Specific (AS) plasmid, which links PETase activity to an observable output for screening.

## PETase Display (PD) Plasmid Design

The PD plasmid contains an ice nucleation protein (INP)-based display system upstream of a BsmBI cloning site, allowing for different PETases to be cloned into the PD vector via Golden Gate Assembly (GGA), and expression of PETases on the outer membrane of transformed cells.

The PD-V1 plasmid was based on AF_lacZ pDest (BBa_J435300), with a tac promoter (BBa_J435360), BC_B0034 RBS (BBa_J435364), and a double terminator composed of rrnB T1 terminator (BBa_J434007) and the T7 terminator from AF_lacZ pDest. This version of the PD plasmid had the PETase display system controlled by IPTG for an early assay concept that was discarded. To simplify the PD plasmid, a second version was made.

The PD-V2 plasmid uses pUC57-Mini-Key IIS Free as its base vector. The promoter-PETase display-terminator unit was adapted from the sequence used in the PD-V1 plasmid. The tac promoter sequence was replaced with the T7 promoter sequence from BBa_K3633015, and the rrnB T1 terminator sequence was replaced with the T7 terminator sequence from the pET-21a(+) vector. The sequences surrounding the promoter and terminator were unmodified. A start codon present between the BC_B0034 RBS and beginning of the INP sequence was also mutated. These modified sequences have been added to the iGEM Registry of Standard Biological Parts. The PETase display system (Figure 2) and the surrounding promoter, RBS, and terminator were oriented in a direction consistent with AmpR and the ori. The PETase display system itself was unchanged.

### PETase Display System Design

The PETase display system uses the glycosylphosphatidylinositol anchoring of INPs to localize PETases to the outer membrane of E. coli, and has previously been well-validated for surface display of other classes of enzymes, such as carboxymethylcellulases and laccases [@pmc91897; @bit10892; @analytica2024; @trends2002; @bioresource2023]

The sequence for the INP was obtained from BBa_K523013.

A 20-residue flexible GS linker (GGSGGGGSGGGGSGGGSGGG) was designed using the principles in Chen et al. 2013 [@chen2013linkers], and fused to the C-terminal end of the INP, followed by a pair of outwards-facing BsmBI restriction sites and a spacer from the pGGASelect vector. The BsmBI sites were arranged so that the vector overhang corresponded to the last four nucleotides of the GS linker, ensuring the GGA “scar” would not impact the reading frame of any PETases cloned in. Downstream of the BsmBI sites, there is a second GS linker (GGGGS) and FLAG tag to allow for verification of PETase surface display. A stop codon was added inside the pGGASelect spacer to prevent the expression of the C-terminal FLAG tag in constructs that don’t contain a PETase, allowing for detection of PETase surface display.

## Assay Specific (AS) Plasmid Design

To connect PETase activity to a visible output, the Biosensor assay and Selection assay use a second plasmid. These AS plasmids are specific to both of these assays, but use a common backbone. They are also conceptually similar: the AS plasmid backbone uses the pCusC (BBa_K1980004) to respond to copper released from digested PET.

pJUMP26-1A was chosen as the basis for the AS backbone due to it being one of the only non-pBR322/ColE1 ori-based backbones in the 2026 iGEM distribution kit. It instead contains a medium copy number p15A ori, which uses a different copy number determination system than the ColE1 ori-based AF lacZ_pDest and pUC57-Mini-Key IIS Free used in V1 and V2 of the PD plasmid, respectively. pJUMP26-1A also contains KanR instead of AmpR, which is used in both versions of the PD plasmid. These differences in ori and selection marker allow the two plasmids to be independently and stably maintained in the same cell. As this is a different selection marker from V1 and V2 of the PD plasmid, this choice allows for selection of double transformants. The BC_B0034 RBS and EF_ECK9600 terminator were selected from the E. coli Protein Expression Toolkit.

### Promoter Selection

Copper was selected as our inducer to allow for future integration into molten PET, as common inducers such as IPTG decompose below the melting point of PET [@sigma2025iptg; @sigma2025pet]. Copper is also an established inducer for S. cerevisiae [@zhou2021copper], allowing for future translation of our assays into yeast.

Two promoters were considered when designing the reporter plasmid. copA and CusC were the two most common promoters in copper systems used in previous literature [@fu2024copper; @yamamoto2005copper]. copA is part of the CueR system, which is a copper efflux regulator. CueR activates transcription through copA in the presence of copper. CusC is part of a similar system with CusCFBA copper efflux, which also activates transcription through CusC in the presence of copper. CusRS is also included in this system, which allows for increased gene expression under high copper conditions.

CusC was determined to be advantageous as it can detect copper in the periplasm, which may be the first location of copper intake into cells [@jbc80206]. In contrast, CueR detects only cytoplasmic copper, which could increase false negatives in the assay if copper is not able to readily enter intracellular locations. CusC also works optimally near toxic concentrations of copper, allowing us to be less concerned about copper toxicity at higher levels. This also makes CusC a less leaky promoter as it requires a higher concentration of copper to be activated. Therefore, pCusC was selected for use in both assays.

## Assembly

The empty vectors were intended to be assembled through GGA using parts from the 2026 iGEM distribution kit and ordered fragments, however the empty AS plasmid fragment was ordered and circularized via KLD reaction to save time. The empty AS plasmid fragment was ordered without pCusC to allow for a different promoter to be used if needed. pCusC, msGFP2, sul1, sul2, and dfrB10 inserts were cloned into the empty AS plasmid by GGA using BsaI to make the various complete AS plasmids. PETase inserts were cloned into the empty PD-V2 plasmid by GGA using BsmBI.
