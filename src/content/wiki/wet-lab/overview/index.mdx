We can now generate enzyme candidates faster than any lab can test them, and with the PETadex, the gap has widened. Finding novel PETases is no longer the hard part, the challenge lies in measuring them in a high-throughput manner. To address this, our Wet Lab team built two novel high-throughput assays: Biosensor and Selection, which have a readout of either fluorescence or survival.

## Why the Field Standard is Not Enough

Existing PETase screens make a crucial tradeoff: substrate fidelity for throughput, or throughput for rigour. Neither is sufficient at the scale required to leverage millions of novel enzymes.

**Plate screens use a PET subunit, not crystalline PET.** BHET is the soluble subunit of PET. The halo assay dissolves BHET into agar and reads the reaction product as a white halo around each colony, establishing that an enzyme is catalytically active. [@ChikhiLoganPlanetaryscale2024]. However, it does not clearly establish whether the enzyme can depolymerize a crystalline polymer—the core claim that matters for plastic waste.

**Quantitative methods do not scale.** HPLC can resolve and quantify the actual degradation products, TPA and MHET, and remains the gold standard for measuring activity [@PirilloAnalyticalMethods2021]. However HPLC limits the throughput, restricting screens to tens of enzymes rather than thousands. The costs of using HPLC ruled it out as a primary screening procedure and drove us to design the Biosensor and Selection assays.

## One Core Mechanism, Two Readouts

Our Biosensor and Selection assays are largely the same as they share a single mechanism.

First, copper is loaded into a PET mesh. When a PETase displayed on the outer membrane of E. coli and hydrolyzes the mesh, there is a physical release of copper into the medium and the periplasm of nearby cells. The released copper activates the copper-responsive CusC promoter (pCusC) [@YamamotoTranscriptional2005; @OuttenIndependent2001]. Copper release is our proxy for degradation, and because the copper sits inside the polymer, copper inducible pathways will only activate if a PETase cuts the polymer itself rather than a soluble subunit.

Depending on the assay, either a GFP or antibiotic resistance gene is induced by pCusC, allowing for easy switching between assays by switching out one gene rather than redesigning the entire system. Screening a surface-displayed enzyme library by its effect on a colony is an established strategy, and has been used to evolve improved cellulase variants [@KimBacterialCell2000]. **Biosensor** puts msGFP2 downstream of pCusC, so an active PETase makes its colony fluoresce [@ValbuenaPhotostable2020]. Whereas, **Selection** puts a sulfonamide resistance gene there instead, so an active PETase enables its colony to grow on antibiotics.
