## Overview

### Problems with the field standard assays

Currently, commonly used assays for PETase testing experiment on the PET subunit substrate, BHET, rather than testing directly on crystalline PET. Surface-expressed PETase on yeast cuts BHET into MHET, which results in visible clearing/halo that can be measured to screen PETases[@chikhi2025logan]. Some other high-throughput assays use HPLC, which is expensive to reproduce.

Many PETase activity assays such as the halo assay use BHET, a subunit of PET, rather than crystalline PET as a substrate. Some other high-throughput assays use HPLC, which is expensive to reproduce.

### Design of our Novel High-Throughput Assays

To assess the activity of PETase, our three assays, Biosensor, Selection, and Dye Release, leverage the fact that PETase breaks down PET, triggering observations that are later quantified to calculate PETase activity. Compared with other PETase testing assays, our assays are cost-effective and high-throughput, while providing reliable quantitative results. With our three assays, we aim to benchmark them against common PETases such as IsPETase and FAST-PETase, obtaining data for quantitative comparisons of PETase activities. This transforms cheap and high-throughput PETase screening from a binary readout into a continuous quantitative readout, and PETase variants can be ranked based on their plastic degradation efficiency
