The Selection assay was designed to identify PETase variants that can successfully break down PET by looking at bacterial growth. This assay uses copper embedded into PET to connect PETase activity to antibiotic resistance. When a PETase breaks down the PET mesh, copper ions are released into the extracellular and periplasmic space of bacteria, which activates pCusC. This then activates transcription of the downstream secondary antibiotic resistance gene, allowing the bacteria to survive in the presence of the antibiotic. If a PETase is unable to break down enough PET to release sufficient copper, the bacteria would not express enough resistance and their growth would be inhibited. Colonies that survive the selection process can then be sequenced to identify the PETase variants they express.

## Resistance Marker

A bacteriostatic antibiotic was selected, as they prevent bacteria from growing rather than directly killing them, providing cells enough time to express PETase, break down PET, release copper, and activate the resistance gene. We investigated two antibiotic options: sulfonamides and trimethoprim. Both interfere with bacterial folate synthesis, but they target different enzymes in the pathway.

For trimethoprim, we chose dfrB10 for its small size and sequence dissimilarity with wild-type E. coli dihydrofolate reductase. However, trimethoprim systems are susceptible to high background escape frequency by spontaneous folA mutations. Because of this, we decided to also use sulfadiazine, from the sulfonamide family, paired with the sul1 and sul2 resistance genes for their higher reliability.

Sul1 and sul2 encode sulfadiazine-resistant variants of dihydropteroate synthase [@venkatesan2023molecular; @ma2021correlation], and dfrB10 encodes a trimethoprim-resistant version of dihydrofolate reductase [@li2023construction]. GGA was attempted with the AS Plasmid vector and all three resistance genes, however only the assembly with sul1 was successful, and so all testing was performed with sul1.

Sulfadiazine and trimethoprim both enact bacteriostatic effect by inhibiting the folate synthesis pathway to cause thymine deprivation, and eventually thymineless death [@gonsalves2024mechanisms]. However, bacterial uptake of exogenous thymidine or thymine can directly bypass the inhibitory effect and cause susceptible bacteria to appear resistant [@wiegand2008agar]. Thymine- and thymidine-free medium must be used to avoid this issue, and we thus selected Mueller-Hinton agar for use in all Selection assays.
